Azure Virtual Desktop / Conditional Access Bypass
Threat actors compromised a financial institution by exploiting trusted Azure Virtual Desktop (AVD) sessions to circumvent Microsoft Entra Conditional Access controls. By operating through an approved virtual desktop environment, attackers appeared to be accessing corporate resources from a trusted device, enabling unauthorized access and large-scale Microsoft 365 data theft. This activity is particularly relevant to North American financial institutions (FIs) and banking organizations that rely on Microsoft cloud infrastructure and identity-based security controls. The incident demonstrates how compromised credentials and trusted sessions can undermine authentication safeguards, potentially exposing sensitive customer information, financial records, and internal communications. Institutions utilizing virtual desktop infrastructure and cloud-based productivity platforms may face similar risks where access policies rely heavily on device trust and established user sessions.

FAMOUS CHOLLIMA
North Korean threat actors associated with FAMOUS CHOLLIMA continue targeting technology professionals through fraudulent recruitment campaigns, leveraging fake job interviews and technical assessments to deceive candidates into executing malicious code. Recent reporting identified a U.S. financial-services organization among affected entities, highlighting the threat's direct relevance to North American FIs and banking institutions. These organizations maintain substantial technical workforces and frequently recruit developers, engineers, and IT specialists who may possess privileged access to sensitive systems. Successful exploitation could enable credential theft, endpoint compromise, and unauthorized access to corporate networks, creating opportunities for financial data theft, operational disruption, and further intrusion into critical banking infrastructure.

ARTEX
Cybercriminals are leveraging AI-assisted reconnaissance and exploitation tools to identify vulnerabilities in internet-facing applications and accelerate intrusion attempts against financial institutions. These capabilities automate portions of vulnerability discovery and exploitation, potentially reducing the time between identifying exposed systems and achieving unauthorized access. Although much of the observed activity occurred outside North America, the targeting of financial organizations globally makes this threat relevant to North American FIs and banking institutions operating similar cloud environments, web applications, and external-facing infrastructure. The increased speed and scalability of AI-assisted attacks may place additional pressure on vulnerability management programs, particularly where remediation timelines and exposure monitoring are insufficient to address rapidly emerging threats.

October Cyber Threat Landscape
October's threat reporting highlights sustained ransomware activity across North America, which accounted for approximately 48% of observed ransomware victims. Financial institutions also face persistent risks involving compromised credentials, identity-based attacks, and sensitive corporate information being submitted to generative AI platforms. These developments are particularly relevant to North American FIs and banking institutions due to their dependence on digital banking platforms, interconnected financial services, and the protection of sensitive customer and transactional data. Successful attacks could disrupt payment processing, compromise customer accounts, expose regulated financial information, and create significant operational and regulatory consequences. The continued prevalence of ransomware and identity-based threats reinforces the importance of protecting critical banking operations and managing risks associated with employee access and emerging technologies.

ScreenConnect / MSP Compromise
Threat actors compromised trusted managed service providers (MSPs) and leveraged legitimate ScreenConnect remote-access software to establish unauthorized access to downstream customer environments. Reported activity affected more than 286 systems across 47 organizations, demonstrating how trusted administrative privileges and remote-support connections can facilitate intrusions beyond the initially compromised provider. This activity is particularly relevant to North American FIs and banking institutions that depend on third-party technology providers for infrastructure management, application support, and operational services. Compromised vendor credentials or remote-management infrastructure could provide attackers with access to sensitive banking systems, customer information, and interconnected enterprise environments without directly exploiting the financial institution's perimeter. The incident highlights the potential for third-party compromises to create broader operational and supply-chain risks across the financial sector.
